# INFRA-1658 — triage of the 55 alerts firing on op-usxpress-dev

**Scope: op-usxpress-dev (10.10.82.50) only, measured 2026-08-24 ~18:55 UTC** via
`scripts/triage-firing-alerts.sh --context admin@op-usxpress-dev`. QA and prod are not
measured. Numbers carry that scope.

55 firing. They are **eleven situations**, not fifty-five problems. Four are current.

---

## ⚠️ Two traps in the data itself

**1. Twenty-six of the 55 have `activeAt` = 2026-08-24T14:49:42, and that is not when they
started.** It is when the kube-state-metrics pod was replaced for the INFRA-1657 CRS change.
All 26 carry `instance=10.244.1.225:8080` — KSM's own address — inside the alert's label
set, so replacing the pod changed every alert instance's identity and reset `activeAt`.

This is the same defect fixed in INFRA-1657, present in kube-prometheus-stack's shipped
rules: any label that identifies the *exporter* rather than the *subject* makes the alert's
age meaningless across an exporter restart.

**Their true ages are unknown, not recent.** Anything below dated 14:49:42 should be read as
"at least since then".

**2. `KubeJobFailed` has no time bound.** `kube_job_failed` stays `1` for as long as the Job
object exists, so a job that failed once in June still fires today. Decoding the cron
suffixes (minutes since epoch × 60):

| Job | Ran |
|---|---|
| `kube-system/cilium-node-reconciler-29697930/945/960/975` | 2026-06-18–19 |
| `ecr-credentials/ecr-credentials-sync-29697230/235/240` | 2026-06-18 |
| `velero/prometheus-default-kopia-nscgh-maintain-job-1782245566631/569761` | ~2026-06-23 |
| `istio-system/istiod-health-check-29768840` | ~2026-08-06 |
| `kube-system/manual-test-1015` | unknown — a hand-run test |

**All eleven are historical.** None indicates a job failing now.

### 1. `Watchdog` — do not touch (1)

Firing since 2026-06-24 because that is its entire purpose: a heartbeat proving the alert
path is alive. It **must** survive into INFRA-1659 and be routed to a dead-man's-switch
receiver. If it ever stops, the pipeline is broken. Nobody should "fix" this.

### 2. Talos control plane — fix the rule (5)

`KubeProxyDown`, `KubeControllerManagerDown`, `KubeSchedulerDown`, `TargetDown`×2
(`kube-system`). All since **2026-06-24T01:35**, i.e. cluster build.

On Talos the control-plane components are static pods that kube-prometheus-stack's default
scrape config never reaches. The rules are measuring their own blind spot: **two months of
`critical` from a rule that cannot succeed.** Three of the five are `critical`, which means
turning on delivery today pages someone immediately for nothing.

Fix in the kube-prometheus-stack values: `kubeProxy.enabled: false`,
`kubeControllerManager.enabled: false`, `kubeScheduler.enabled: false` — or expose the real
endpoints deliberately, which on Talos is a decision, not a config line. Choosing to disable
means accepting we do not monitor those components; that trade-off should be recorded.

### 3. Stale failed Jobs — clean up, then bound the rule (11)

See the table above. Two separate pieces of work:

- **Delete the stale Job objects.** `manual-test-1015` should not exist at all.
- **Stop them accumulating**: set `ttlSecondsAfterFinished` on the CronJobs, and
  `failedJobsHistoryLimit: 1`. Without that this recurs.

The Velero pair is the one to actually look at before deleting — `kopia maintain` failing
means backup-repository maintenance did not run in June. That may be long resolved, but
"backups" is not something to delete an alert about without checking.

### 4. `attrition-api` — real, two months, needs an owner (8)

`KubePodNotReady` ×2 (`attrition-api-5f95f79d46-xbhf4` since **2026-06-24**,
`attrition-api-858874df66-bd4lb` since 2026-08-22), `KubeContainerWaiting` ×3 across three
different ReplicaSets, `KubeDeploymentReplicasMismatch`, `KubeDeploymentRolloutStuck`,
`KubePdbNotEnoughHealthyPods` (`attrition-api-chart`).

Three ReplicaSets with waiting containers is a deployment that has been retried repeatedly
and never come up. **This is an application, not platform** — it needs the app team, and the
`attrition` namespace's ownership should be established before delivery is switched on, or
these eight alerts go to platform and stay there.

### 5. `wiz-sensor` — silence with an expiry (10)

`KubePodNotReady` ×7 (since 2026-07-21), `KubeDaemonSetRolloutStuck wiz/wiz-sensor`,
`FluxKustomizationFailed flux-system/wiz-sensor`, `FluxHelmReleaseFailed wiz/wiz-sensor`.

Known and correct: INFRA-1586, blocked on a real Wiz token from Steve. **Silence with an
expiry, not a fix.** A silence without an expiry is deleting the alert slowly. Suggested
expiry 2026-09-30, which forces the token conversation to happen.

### 6. `risingwave-2` — retire the rules with the namespace (8)

`PodNotReadyPlatformNS`, `PodCrashLoopBackOffPlatformNS`, `KubePodCrashLooping`,
`KubePersistentVolumeFillingUp` (all `prometheus-server-7547c5b58d-npb88`, PVC full
2026-08-18), `KubeDeploymentReplicasMismatch`, `KubeDeploymentRolloutStuck`,
`FluxHelmReleaseFailed risingwave-2/prometheus`, `FluxKustomizationFailed
flux-system/risingwave`.

`risingwave-2` is a platform test bed that is **not** going to QA or prod. Our own
`platform-alerts.yaml` hardcodes it: `GhostunnelDown` selects
`namespace="risingwave-2", deployment="ghostunnel-rw2-sql"`, and the namespace appears in the
`PodCrashLoopBackOffPlatformNS` / `PodNotReadyPlatformNS` regexes.

**Retire, do not silence.** Silencing something that is never coming back means carrying it
forever. If the namespace is being kept for now, then the decision is to delete these rule
selectors when it goes — and that should be written on the ticket that deletes it.

### 7. Ceph — real (2)

`CephClusterHealthWarn` since **2026-08-17T03:21** (ours), and
`KubePdbNotEnoughHealthyPods rook-ceph/rook-ceph-rgw-object-store`. The RGW PDB not being
satisfied is a genuine availability gap in object storage. Ceph pool usage was 26% on
2026-08-21, so this is not capacity. Needs `ceph health detail`.

### 8. Istio DaemonSets stuck — real (2)

`KubeDaemonSetRolloutStuck` on `istio-system/ztunnel` and `istio-system/istio-cni-node`.
Both are ambient-mode components. A stuck CNI DaemonSet is not cosmetic — it affects pod
networking on whichever nodes did not roll. Age unknown (reset by the KSM restart).

Also here: `KubeDaemonSetRolloutStuck` + `KubeDaemonSetNotScheduled` on
`monitoring/prometheus-prometheus-node-exporter`. Note the namespace — the live stack runs
`prometheus-stack-prometheus-node-exporter` in `prometheus`. **`monitoring` is a leftover
from an earlier install.** Delete the namespace's remnants; counted under #3 as cleanup.

### 9. `ClusterDNSUnreachable` — verify before deciding (1)

Ours, `critical`, firing since **2026-06-24T01:40:17** — cluster build. Either cluster DNS
has been unreachable for two months, which contradicts everything else working, or the rule
is wrong in the same way as #2. **Do not classify this until the expression is read and the
metric queried.** It is the only alert here where both answers are alarming.

### 10. `PrometheusNotConnectedToAlertmanagers` — correct (1)

Since 2026-06-24. This is INFRA-1659 reporting its own absence, accurately, for two months.
It resolves itself the moment delivery exists.

### 11. `CPUThrottlingHigh` — drop (4)

Four instances, all `node-exporter` containers, `info` severity. The upstream default is
widely considered noise; node-exporter throttling on a CPU limit is expected and harmless.
Raise the limit or drop the rule for this container.

## What this means for INFRA-1659

**Delivering today would page someone immediately for four things that are not true**
(#2, three of them `critical`) **and eleven that finished in June** (#3). That is how an
alerting channel dies in its first week.

Order is: #2 and #3 first (they are the noise), then #5 and #6 (silence and retire), then
turn on delivery, with #4, #7, #8 and #9 as real work that should have gone somewhere.
